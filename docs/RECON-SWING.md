# RECON-SWING: shallow native nets for the T5 clustering kernels (2026-09-30)

Question: GRAPH-SWING showed the T3 graph loss was the author, not the medium. Does the same hold for the reconciliation
core, whose heart is connected components with canonical id = largest member (union-find in the spec), and does a
shallow net scale toward G4 (100k+ nodes)?

Method: one Opus-class author, hand-written HVM2 (about 2.5 hours), nothing searched or learned. Nets are in
`runs/exp10/<prog>.hvm`, generated by `runs/exp10/build.py` from the templates in `runs/exp10/lib_ext.py` (which reuses
`stream`/`nav` and the trie primitives of `runs/exp5`). Every net passes the hidden suite on seeds 0, 1 and 2 (51/51 runs,
`runs/exp10/verify_all.log`; verified with `genome.verify.verify` at 3 workers via `runs/exp10/v.py`). Metrics below are the
verifier's seed-0 medians over the large cases (n = 72, 144). "prev native" and "Bend" are the frozen author's accepted nets,
combined exactly as `genome/exp_quick.py` does (g1, g1r1, probe2, g1esc layers).

## Design

**1. Pre-wired channels instead of per-round keyed routing (the new idea).** GRAPH-SWING's components net re-emitted
every improved label through keyed trie updates each round (about 150-300 depth per round, dominated by the gated keyed
emission). Here the edge list is read once; for each edge (u, v) the walker creates two fresh wires c1, c2 and pushes
the channel ends `(in out) = (c2 c1)` onto vertex u and `(c1 c2)` onto vertex v (pre-expanding keyed pushes, as in
GRAPH-SWING). A channel is a stream: the producer writes `o ~ (x next)`, the consumer matches `i ~ (y rest)`, and since
both ends are one wire a single CON~CON annihilation moves the value and hands both sides the next round's wire. After
setup, a round involves no trie routing at all: each vertex walks its own channel list (send x, receive y, running max),
and the "where does this value go" question was answered once, at setup, by the wiring. This is idea 3 of GRAPH-SWING
(wires as return addresses) made persistent across rounds.

**2. No global barrier: persistent processes and a lagged decision stream.** With channels, a synchronous round still
cost about 105 depth, mostly the trie descent and the global OR/decision. `cc_pipe` turns every vertex into a persistent
process that loops on its own; per-node up-loops OR the change flags (tagged streams), the root turns flag j into
decision j+k, and per-node down-loops broadcast decisions. A vertex waits for decision j only before its round j+k, so the
~4L-deep reduce/broadcast latency overlaps k rounds of useful work. Speculative rounds are idempotent (LP has converged),
so they cost work, not correctness. Measured on a 32-edge path: 105 -> 21 depth per round (k=4). On the test
distribution k=2 is best (k=1: 806, k=2: 656, k=3: 686 median depth on cluster_count before later tweaks).

**3. Small things that paid.** Inline switch branches (no REF expansion) in the per-channel max; rejected candidates
(score < tau) push nothing: the accept bit rides in the payload and the push action drops it at the leaf, so no switch
sits on the stream's critical chain; min-label (for `label` and `list`) is the same core with `mn` and a neutral of
16777215.

**Outputs from the final label trie** (all O(L)-deep traversals after LP): tail-threaded lists (canon, label, emit,
members_of), counts (count, merge_count = n - #components), a scatter-count trie of cluster sizes via pre-expanding
keyed increments (sizes, histogram as an 8-vector reduction, largest, count_ge, closure_pairs = sum s(s-1)/2), bucket
lists keyed by min-label threaded right-to-left so members come out ascending (list), keyed reads for queries (canon_of,
same: the query stream runs in parallel with the edge stream and pre-builds its chain of reads), and a 16-bit word bitset
over u*2^L+v for distinct pairs (implied_unproposed = closure - distinct). Singletons needs no LP: n minus the number of
distinct endpoints.

**Reusable templates** (`runs/exp10/lib_ext.py`, docstrings give ports and cost): `cc_core(p, op, neutral)` (channels,
synchronous rounds), `cc_pipe(p, op, neutral, k)` (channels, pipelined termination; used by all nets), `tl_leafmap`,
`INC`/`scat` (scatter-count), `get` (keyed read), `mk`, `cp`/`cq` push actions; plus `ttl`/`tred` generic trie walkers in
`build.py`.

## Depth vs work: where the depth goes now

depth ~ 2.3*m (reading the candidate list; the list is a linked spine, 2 rounds/cell is a hard floor) + ~250 (lg, trie
setup, spawn, output) + (R + k) * T_leaf, where R = LP rounds (max distance from a vertex to its component's largest
member) and T_leaf ~ 15 + 4-6 * deg_max. Work ~ (R + k + 1) * (n + m) * ~25 plus O(2^L) setup.

Algorithms considered and why this one:
- Label propagation over tries (GRAPH-SWING cc_count): R rounds x 150-300 depth. Replaced.
- Shiloach-Vishkin / FastSV (hooking + pointer jumping): O(log n) rounds, but every round needs data-dependent gathers
  (f[f[u]]) and scatters, i.e. keyed trie operations whose control flow waits for the data (~8 depth per trie level per
  hop, 3-4 such operations per round): roughly 300-500 depth per round. At these sizes (R = 1-14) and at the measured
  sizes up to 8,192 (R = 17) the channel LP at ~20-100 per round wins; SV only wins when R exceeds about 5 * log2 n,
  i.e. long chains (a 1,000-long path).
- A pure dataflow net (no rounds) is impossible for components: the fixpoint is cyclic, and without labelled DUPs a
  round function containing DUPs cannot be copied, so the per-round structure must be regenerated (here: by each vertex
  process walking its own channel list).
- Termination: a global OR is unavoidable without knowing the diameter; the lagged decision stream hides it.

Honest limits: rounds are still R + k (long chains cost R rounds; a 32-long path costs about 21 per round), the high-degree
vertex walks its channels sequentially (a degree-16 star costs about 100 per round; bucketing channels by neighbour bits or
a balanced channel tree would cut this to O(log deg)), and cluster-size scatter serialises increments on a hot key (sizes is
~500 deeper than canon when a giant component forms).

## Scaling (t5_cluster_canon net, depth oracle)

Inputs: the corpus generator's world (true clusters of 1-12 plus noisy random candidates), m = 2n candidates accepted at
tau = 600 (`runs/exp10/scale.py`, outputs checked against the reference).

| n | accepted edges m | LP rounds R | max degree | depth | interactions | depth / node | interactions / node | output correct |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128 | 138 | 5 | 8 | 979 | 220,615 | 7.65 | 1,724 | yes |
| 512 | 538 | 8 | 9 | 2,240 | 1,158,773 | 4.38 | 2,263 | yes |
| 2,048 | 2,142 | 14 | 13 | 7,018 | 6,676,537 | 3.43 | 3,260 | yes |
| 8,192 | 8,660 | 17 | 13 | 22,872 | 31,535,435 | 2.79 | 3,850 | yes |

(`runs/exp10/scale_depth.log`; correctness checked through the verifier's digest path.) Depth fits
depth ~ 2.4 * m + (R + k) * ~100 + ~300 to within 3% at n >= 2,048 (the fixed part is smaller at n = 128-512).

Reading: depth per node falls toward the input floor (about 2.3 rounds per accepted edge, ~2.5 per node here), because the
LP part grows only with R (5 -> 17 rounds) while the list read grows with m. Interactions per node grow with R (each round
touches every vertex and channel): ~1.7k -> 3.8k per node.

Extrapolation to 100k nodes (same generator, m ~ 1.05n accepted): depth ~ 2.3 * 106k + (R + k) * ~120 ~ 250k-260k rounds,
over 95% of it the sequential read of the input list; interactions ~ 100k * ~4.5k ~ 450M (R ~ 20-25), and the live net is O(n + m) nodes, far under the 2^29-node cap.

Wall clock. The native arm64 path (`genome.executor.run_native`: `hvm gen-c` + clang) did not produce a number: gen-c
compiles the input book into C, and at n = 2,048 that is a 3.8 MB C file (from a 46 KB net) that clang -O1 had not finished
compiling after about 10 minutes on the loaded machine (the -O3 attempt at n = 8,192 likewise ran over 10 minutes); I stopped it at the time box. Fallback, the
pinned Rust interpreter (`hvm run`, x86_64 under Rosetta, machine heavily loaded): n = 8,192 ran 31.5M interactions in
2.74 s (11.5 MIPS). At n = 32,768 the interpreter aborts with a stack overflow in its own (recursive) parsing/readback of
the 34k-edge input and 32k-element output, before any question of HVM2's 2^29-node heap. So the largest measured size
is 8,192. Extrapolating the measured interactions per node (~4.5k at R ~ 20-25): 100k nodes ~ 450M interactions, about
40 s on this interpreter or ~0.6 s at the ~800 MIPS native peak recorded in gates/STATUS.md. To time the native path at
scale, the input must not be compiled into C (pass it as data to a precompiled runtime); that is a harness change, not a
net change.

## Verdict

Components with canonical id = largest member, the core of reconciliation, is shallow on HVM2 in the part the medium
controls: after the input is read, a round costs about 20-100 depth regardless of n, and the whole LP adds (R + k) of
them. On the T5 sizes the new nets are 4.5-629x shallower than Bend and 6-14,000x shallower than the frozen native nets,
with 14 of 17 also cheaper in interactions. At 100k+ nodes the remaining obstacle is not the algorithm but the task
interface: the candidate list arrives as a linked list, and any consumer needs 2 rounds per cell to reach cell i, so depth
is Theta(m) no matter how the rest is built. Shallow reconciliation at 100k+ is feasible on HVM2 if G4 feeds candidates as
a balanced structure (a tree of chunks, or pre-bucketed by vertex id), which G4 controls since it encodes the replayed
data. Then depth would be ~ log(m) + (R + k) * T_leaf, on the order of a few thousand rounds for OpenSanctions-like
clusters (largest 81, so R <= 80, typically far less), with work ~ (R + k) * (n + m).

## Next step

1. Tree-shaped input for G4 (candidate chunks as a balanced tree, or a vertex-keyed trie of candidate lists) and a
   parallel reader; this is the single change that turns Theta(m) depth into polylog + rounds.
2. The order-dependent greedy with must-not-link (t5_reconcile_canon_mnl / _full, conflict_greedy*): sequential by
   specification. A shallow route: run unconstrained LP, then only clusters that contain a must-not-link pair (27 in the
   whole OpenSanctions file) need the ordered greedy, which can run per conflicted cluster in parallel.
3. Channel buckets (keyed by low bits of the neighbour) or balanced channel trees to cut T_leaf on hubs; hooking +
   shortcutting on top of channels for long-chain inputs.
