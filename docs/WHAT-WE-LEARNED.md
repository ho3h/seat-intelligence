# What we learned (2026-09-28 to 2026-09-30), honestly scored

Companion to gates/STATUS.md and docs/SWINGS.md. Every number here was produced by the executor-as-verifier; where a claim comes only from an agent's report it is marked.

## The thesis, restated after the evidence
Original: machine authors writing directly in a graph medium beat the same authors writing through a human language, and what they write becomes a language.
After the evidence: **the medium is writable and has one real, narrow, automatable advantage; the big lever is parallel algorithm design, which is medium-independent; authors fail on program structure, and structure is best supplied by verified reusable words.**

## Established (checked)
1. **Frozen models can write correct nets.** G0 20/20 (Opus). G1 seed 0: native 176/200 (88%), Bend 189/200 (94.5%) with frozen Luna Pro (+DeepSeek escalation); tree/language tier 40/40 native vs 31/40 Bend.
2. **Native wins are tier-shaped.** Lists, sorting, trees: native wins 77-94%, median depth gain about +21%. Graphs (T3) and reconciliation (T5) as written by the frozen authors: native loses (49%, 63%).
3. **The graph loss was an author/design problem.** A strong author's redesigns of 8 graph programs (swing 8) were 2.9x-107x shallower than the frozen author's Bend. Independently audited (seeds 0-2, 64x inputs, native C wall clock) for the programs checked.
4. **But two thirds of that was algorithm, not medium (swing 12).** A strong Bend author ported the ideas and closed the depth gap from 10.9x to 2.2x (geomean); interactions within 1.04-1.26x of native. The claim "3-107x better than Bend" does not survive a fair baseline.
5. **The medium-specific residual is one thing: the K-cell lookahead walker** (about 2.3 rounds/element vs about 9 for a one-switch-per-cell walk); Bend's match cannot express it. **It is now applied automatically (swing 18):** 51/76 nets transformed, 232/232 pass seeds 0-2, median depth -43.5%, native vs strong Bend 1.09x -> 2.22x shallower. (Spot-check by lead: 8/8 pass fresh seeds; gains range -61% to 0% depending on what bounds the walker.)
6. **Wiring is a library problem (swing 13).** 24 tested primitives; 8/8 blind t3_a programs composed, pass seeds 0-2 first try; abstraction cost +1.7%; about 69% of T3/T5 composable now, about 86% with about 5 more primitives.
7. **Enumeration fills the holes in verified words with no language model (swing 22).** 81% solved from 3 examples, 99% with counterexample feedback, on in-family generated tasks; 66% coverage with the pre-registered core words (100% only after extending the library toward uncovered shapes).
8. **Scale-out of strong authorship:** 21 more t3_b programs and 17 T5 clustering kernels hand-designed, all pass seeds 0-2 (agent reports; partial lead audits). Reconciliation depth per node falls with size; the linked-list input makes depth linear in edges, so real-scale runs need tree-shaped input.
9. **Robustness:** 38/40 frozen-author nets stay correct at 64x input size (2 runtime crashes on T5, not wrong answers). Native nets are not transcriptions of the Bend route (structural overlap ratio 1.15).

## Closed as negatives (kill rules hit)
- Interface tricks: mechanical wire repair rescues 3%; constrained decoding +0.3pt (CI includes 0); the model's mistake is planning the dataflow, not writing wires.
- Factorized generation (skeleton by LM + wiring by matcher): wiring given structure is learnable (38.9% net exec vs 1.3% random) but end to end worse than plain (8.8% vs 14.4%): LM errors are structural.
- Small-model plain SFT: Qwen3-1.7B 23.8% on its own training tasks; 4B 31% (Bend adapter 88%). Expert iteration made it worse (no control).
- Executor-guided search for speed: 10-15% in both arms, mostly inlining; random mutation about 0. Inlining is not fully exact under unlabeled DUPs (one seed 1-2 failure).
- Depth advantage does not grow with input size (ratio 0.99, n=32 to 512): constant factor.
- Superposition search on HVM4 (twice): exact but gain 0.7x-7.5x vs interpreted, about 1x vs compiled; a plain bottom-up enumerator with observational-equivalence merging wins 100-10,000x. Lesson: HVM SUP lacks value merging.
- Original held-out transfer test was invalid (unseen types, no encodings in prompt); retracted.

## Update 2026-09-30 (late): the fair comparison on T3/T5 (fair-Bend swarm, runs/exp17)
Strong-author Bend (Opus agents, same effort as the native redesigns, allowed to use the design docs) vs strong-author native nets on the 87 T3/T5 programs where both pass (seeds 0 and 1 for Bend; native re-verified seeds 3,4; provisional, agents may still be tuning):
- Depth Bend/native: geomean 0.56x, median 0.91x. Native shallower on 39, Bend shallower on 48. T3: native shallower on 18/44 (41%); T5: 21/43 (49%).
- Interactions Bend/native: geomean 1.03x, median 1.27x (native does about 21% less work at the median).
- CONCLUSION: the earlier "native wins 88% / median 34% on T3/T5" was an author effect (strong author vs weak author's Bend). With equal effort the depth advantage on graph/reconciliation programs is gone. What remains: (a) lists/sort/trees where frozen vs frozen native wins 77-94% (median depth +21%); (b) the K-cell lookahead residual (about 2x depth on list walks), now automatic; (c) about 21% fewer interactions on T3/T5.
- Also: reconciliation net at 100k real entities is exact but union-find is about 3,650x faster (C): speed is not the case for this workload.
- Not shown: any across-the-board advantage of nets over a fair human-language route. Shown: nets are writable, verifiable, composable from a checked vocabulary, and have a few real medium-specific advantages.

## FINAL fair-Bend tally (2026-09-30, all 4 groups collected; runs/exp17/results_group*.json vs runs/g1_recount.json)
All 87 strong-author Bend programs pass seeds 0 and 1 (Bend re-verified by the swarm agents; native side from g1_recount, which predates the 7 exp18 T5 order-dependent nets).
- Depth Bend/native: geomean 0.55x, median 0.89x. Native shallower on 39/87, Bend shallower on 48/87. T3: native shallower 18/44; T5: 21/43.
- Interactions Bend/native: geomean 1.06x, median 1.28x (native about 22% less work at the median). T3 median 0.95x (a wash); T5 median 1.48x.
- Every Bend program is 20x-geomean shallower than the frozen-author Bend, so the earlier native "win" on T3/T5 was an author effect, confirmed at n=87.
- Transferred design ideas (native -> Bend): unscoped-lambda channels between vertices, gated dataflow, rootless tree DP, meet-in-the-middle, keyed updates on tuple trees. Bend cannot beat about 9 rounds/list cell (the lookahead residual).
- T5 coverage swing (exp18): 7 order-dependent kernels (greedy merge, conflicts, canonical, property merge) now have native nets passing seeds 0-2 (21/21). On big cases 14x-473x shallower than the frozen-author Bend and 1.5x-5.3x fewer interactions, but no strong-Bend comparison yet for these 7 (swing 27 in progress); greedy is sequential by spec, about 40 rounds/candidate.
- Framing consequence: not an across-the-board speed advantage. Supported claims are writability, verification, composition from a checked vocabulary, lists/trees/lookahead medium advantages.
- Group 3 final report (agent claim, results already in the tally above): 21/21 also pass seed 2; geomean 29x shallower than frozen-author Bend; 8/21 shallower than frozen native. Carried-over ideas: vertex-keyed tuple tries in the list walk, lagged stop decisions, wires as return addresses, Boruvka + pointer jumping. Lessons: a data-dependent `if` in the list walk doubles its depth; lookups in a shared trie copy the whole trie. 12/21 sit within 1.5x of the ~9 rounds/cell Bend floor.
- Group 0 final report (agent claim; results already in the tally): 22/22 pass seeds 0-2; geomean 17.9x shallower than frozen-author Bend (25x excluding 3 reused exp7 programs), interactions 0.42x of old; at parity with hand-written native on 13 programs (geomean 0.96x). Prim and lexicographic topo order still need n sequential steps; Boruvka untried there. ## Swing 21 (stage-level factorization with a small model; agent report, seed-1 audit 40/40, docs/STAGE-FACTORIZATION.md)
Small models emit a short stage-word program; verified templates + fixed glue build the net. Covered in-distribution tasks: 0.6B/1.7B/4B = 100/99.8/100% pass@1 (vs 14.4% for the plain fine-tuned 4B that writes whole nets; paired gain +85.6 pts). Depth cliff gone because glue composes. ASTERISK: task text is generated from the same stage list the vocabulary mirrors (a regex parser scores 100%), the vocabulary is hand-designed and covers 4 of 14 task families (no tree/tuple/graph), and unseen wording is the real problem (0.6B 43%, 1.7B 78%, 4B 67%; 93% after adding three wordings to training). Not evidence yet for a vocabulary grown from use.

## Swing 27 (G4 fairness, agent report; docs/RECON-BEND.md, runs/exp21)
Strong-Bend reconciliation matches the native net at 2k-100k: at 100k core depth 1.02x and interactions 1.34x of native; end-to-end 0.90x depth and 0.99x interactions (both inside the 1.5x kill line, so no G4 advantage over a human-language route). Wall-clock C runtime at 100k is parity within noise (load 5-9). Exact against Python union-find at every N. Bend's unscoped lambdas express the native design (per-edge channels, probes, knot feeding the stop decision). Caveats: one author wrote both arms and Bend reuses the native design ideas; one depth-oracle anomaly (bare-output end-to-end stops early, reported with output folded to a digest); 8-thread C runtime flaky for both arms (2/33 Bend hangs, 2/21 native wrong results); oracle points near the 05:00 pkill were re-run and reproduced.

## Prior-art note on "enumeration with observational equivalence beats HVM superposition" (2026-09-30, web check)
Not a new idea. Bottom-up enumeration with observational-equivalence pruning is the standard technique (Udupa et al. TRANSIT 2013, Albarghouthi et al. Escher 2013, later Probe/others; 2026 miniKanren work ports "prune + bank" to relational search). Taelin's SupGen shares computation across candidate programs with SUP nodes and prunes ill-typed candidates, but a distilled reference VM (SupVM gist) does no merging of equal results; our measurement is consistent with that, but I have not read HVM4's collapser or SupGen source, so "HVM lacks value merging" is unconfirmed for the real system. Our contribution is only a head-to-head measurement (100-10,000x on our task set), not a technique.

## Swing 26 (recipes with typed holes; agent report, runs/exp20, docs/RECIPES.md section 6)
Kill rule (>=20 pts) not hit: Haiku with the recipe library (B) passed 10/16 held-out T5 programs on seeds 0-2 vs 1/16 with checked glue + primitives only (A); paired B-only 10, A-only 1, one-sided sign test p=0.006. Caveats: all 16 held-out are T5 (no T3 covered); sort_by and take_first were added after the agent read the held-out contracts (without the 4 programs that depended on them: 6/12 vs 1/12); harness deviations favoured A (more nudges: 13 A runs vs 5 B runs); hand-classified failures. Weak authors still fail at hole-filling (padding through predicates, pair keys too wide, sum-0 vs absent), unspelled algorithms, and trie sizing. Estimate: ~20 recipes for T5, 40-50 for ~90% of T3+T5. This is the strongest evidence yet that moving design skill into a checked library lifts weak authors.

## HERO-3 verified auto-parallelizer (agent report; docs/HERO-3.md, runs/hero3, kill rule frozen in PREREG.json)
Kill rule not hit. 44-fold benchmark (31 associative, 12 non-associative, 1 planted point bug). Tester accepts 31/31 associative and rejects 12/12 non-associative (3 tester seeds, ~1.06M checks). 32/32 accepted rewrites pass genome.verify seeds 0-2 and a fresh-seed audit; all 12 forced rewrites of rejected folds fail the verifier. On balanced tree input at 1k/10k/100k, fitted depth exponent median 0.109 (max 0.112) vs 1.000 for the list walker; at 100k 2,341-4,079x shallower (median 2,812x) for 1.33x median more interactions. Folding the same tree in order gives exponent 0.997, so the win is from associativity, not the tree. Strong Bend on 5 folds ties (Bend/native depth 1.06x geomean): value is the automation, not the medium. CAVEATS: combiner is supplied (not inferred); the tester is statistical (finds ~1-in-1000 bugs, not 1-in-a-million; missed the planted point bug); the win needs a caller-supplied balanced tree; from a linked list the gain is a constant (list-to-tree conversion costs 7.5 rounds/element, 2.4 with the K=16 lookahead pass, exponent still ~0.97); skewed trees are worse than lists.

Open-wire reduction to canonical form proved 91/92 known-equal pairs (84/92 = 91.3% with the exact tier S alone; the one miss is dead-code elimination); real repo pairs 46/46 (opt.py outputs vs originals 28/28, glue vs hand-wired 2/2, recipe wrappers vs expanded 16/16). 0/46 unequal pairs proved (including 6 rare-witness mutants). ## HERO-5 explain-by-trace (agent report; docs/HERO-5.md, runs/hero5; 100 decisions frozen before tracing, sha256 3f47748260925b29e65bef82872acb2946d827eb1feca12f5929b7aafc16c1d6)
Kill rule hit for the trace itself: 0/100 explanations read straight off the reduction are exact (provenance policy F, data+control: 100 over-inclusive, causal set = whole input; policy D, data only: 100 incomplete, causal set empty; same on a second label-propagation net). The tracer is faithful (traced runs reproduce interaction count and depth exactly on 30/30 problems, overhead ~1.3x wall-clock, noisy). What DOES work, without any trace: delta debugging by re-execution through the real executor with the real blocking rules kept ("context-preserving") gives 100/100 exact explanations (plain delta debugging 99/100; leave-one-out only 60/100), median 20 reruns per decision on the hero chart and 70 on 2,000-record problems (~2 s and ~17 s replayed). A kernel-aware generator gives 100/100 with or without a trace. So the reusable finding is 'exact explanations come from counterfactual re-execution, which is generic and needs no tracer'. CAVEATS: 'exact' judged with only the explanation's own facts present (61/100 also have every fact necessary in the full input because many decisions have redundant evidence); the reading of the kill rule was fixed after seeing dev-set traces (disclosed); one net family plus a size probe; the generator is written for this kernel; saturation cause not isolated (likely sequential greedy state / broadcast relabel).

## HERO-4 vocabulary grown after training, prompt-only (agent report; docs/HERO-4.md, runs/hero4; test set v1 frozen sha256 a4785a7d... before any model run)
Verdict PARTIAL (not cleanly passed by its own pre-registered rule). Base: 5 seating words, Qwen3-1.7B tuned on those only (with alias names for base words). 10 new words authored one at a time (limit, pair, apart, vip, stagger, headseat, bigfirst, waitlist, snake, sectionlead); every word passed genome.verify seeds 0-2 (153/153 runs; 11/11 deliberately wrong nets rejected). Adding a word = signature + one-line gloss + one worked example in the prompt, no retraining. Test wording from independent subagent writers who never saw the signatures. Results (30 tasks x 4 samples per family): 6 of 10 families >=70% pass@1 (exactly the minimum): pair 91.7, apart 79.2, vip 100, stagger 88.3, headseat 95.0, bigfirst 80.0; failing: waitlist 57.5, snake 25.8, limit 10.0, sectionlead 0.0. Replication on fresh test v2 (new writers/parameters): 6 of 10 again (waitlist 69.2, just under). Pooled 62.8% with the word vs 3.2% without (+59.5 pts paired, CI 53.7-65.4). Only 4 of the 6 passing families have CI lower bound >=70. Base regression with all ten words in one prompt: -9.2 pts (CI -13.8 to -4.7) on v1, -8.0 on v2, which breaks the pre-registered 5-point limit; one word at a time: 0.0 (CI -0.7 to +0.8, arm added post-hoc). Look-alike new words displace base ones (53 of 65 failing base samples contained a new word). Failures: snake and sectionlead take the section size as their only number so the model drops the closing line (word line itself correct 91%/97%; appending the closing line would give 82.5%/86.7%); limit needs the size twice. Controls: untuned 4B-Instruct with all 15 words 31.6% pooled (1 of 10); tuned without alias names 37.4% (2 of 10); RETRAINING the 1.7B with the ten words: 98.2% pooled, 10 of 10, base 99.3%. So retraining clearly beats prompt-only, including the four words prompt-only cannot do. Authoring cost per word: ~28 s, 1.0 verifier attempts, ~4 net lines, ~170 tokens; does not fall from word 1 to word 10 (after 223 lines of reusable fragments built first, not charged); real marginal cost was ~30 min of entry rewriting for failing words with no convergence. Caveats: several 'new' words are variants of base mechanics (vip = threshold on order rank, stagger = spread by company, bigfirst = group company ordered by size); words are exact-by-reference heuristics, not solvers; 23 of 300 new-family tasks are no-ops; task is rule-text-to-words translation, not seating a real chart; everything after the first failure census is post-hoc.
